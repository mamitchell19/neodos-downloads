NEW IN 3.5: processors that choose their own speed and report their temperature
  * On Intel processors from about 2015 on (Skylake and later - ThinkPad P51/P52, T470, X1 Carbon 5th generation
    and newer) NeoDOS lets the processor pick its speed itself, many times a second: slow and cool when there is
    little to do, full speed with turbo when a program needs it. Without this the processor stays at the one
    speed the PC's firmware chose. CPU shows the speed range. Normally (CPU SPEED AUTO) a laptop favours saving
    power while it runs on battery and is balanced while the power adapter is plugged in - it switches by itself
    within 10 seconds. CPU SPEED FAST, BALANCED or SAVE picks one for good (until CPU SPEED AUTO). On older Intel
    processors CPU SPEED sets their energy hint. Virtual machines and AMD processors are left as they are. If a PC
    misbehaves, the boot option speed=fixed (or safe) leaves the speed exactly as the firmware set it.
  * Processors with nothing to do now really rest: on a PC with several processors, the idle ones switch their
    timer off instead of waking up 100 times a second, and are woken the moment there is work for them. CPU
    shows how many are resting; CPU IDLE HLT (or idle=hlt) keeps the timers running if a PC misbehaves.
  * CPU and POWER show the processor's temperature from its own thermometer (Intel processors since about 2006),
    also on laptops whose firmware reports no temperatures, and say plainly when it is hot or slowing itself
    down because of the heat (then check the fan and the air vents).

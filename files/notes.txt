NEW IN 3.8: steadier start-up on real PCs
  * PCs with a lot of memory (64 GB or more, such as a ThinkPad P52 with 128 GB) place some devices - the sound
    chip, the USB 3 controller, the network chip, the screen's brightness control - above the first 4 GB. NeoDOS
    now uses their full address there; before, it could crash during start-up on such a PC.
  * If a processor lists a feature that the PC's firmware has locked, NeoDOS now leaves that feature alone instead
    of stopping (processor speed control, energy hint, temperature sensor).
  * The "Safe mode" start-up entry now really leaves the processor's speed and deep sleep to the firmware, as
    the boot options safe, idle=hlt and speed=fixed always promised (they were read too late before).
